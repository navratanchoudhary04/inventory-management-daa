Design & Analysis of Algorithms — Project Report

Inventory Management Optimization
via Heuristic-Seeded Genetic Algorithm

Subject: Design & Analysis of Algorithms  |  Faculty Guide: Ms. Manya  |  April 2026

2. Abstract

This project presents a Genetic Algorithm for Inventory Management Optimization across 500 items. The core innovation is EOQ-Seeded Heuristic Initialization: 30% of the initial population is seeded using Q* = √(2DS/h) with ±10% Gaussian noise. Two further modifications (Gaussian mutation, convergence-based stopping) complement the primary change. The Modified GA achieves lower cost, converges ~38% faster, and executes in measurably less time.

3. Problem Statement

For N=500 items, minimize total annual inventory cost by choosing optimal Order Quantity Q and Reorder Point R for each item:

TotalCost = Σᵢ [ (Dᵢ×365/Qᵢ)×Sᵢ + (Qᵢ/2+ssᵢ)×hᵢ×365 + pᵢ×max(0,Rᵢ−Dᵢ×Lᵢ) ]
Constraints: Qᵢ≥1, Rᵢ≥safetyStockᵢ

5. Methodology

Step 1: Generate 500 items (seeded PRNG)
Step 2: Initialize → Original: 100% random / Modified: 30% EOQ±10%
Step 3: Evaluate fitness for each chromosome
Step 4: Tournament Selection (k=3)
Step 5: Uniform Crossover (rate=0.85)
Step 6: Mutation → Original: random / Modified: Gaussian
Step 7: Elitism — top 5 carry over
Step 8: Termination → fixed / convergence-based
Step 9: Output 500 optimized [Q*,R*] pairs

6. Modification Analysis

A: EOQ-Seeded Initialization (Primary)

Q* = √(2DS/h) gives the analytical optimum for single-item models. Seeding 30% of population near this value reduces the "genetic distance" from the optimum.

Justification: EOQ bridges analytical optimization with metaheuristic search — giving the GA a head start while noise preserves diversity.

B: Gaussian Mutation

Q_new = max(1, Q_old × N(1, 0.15)) — small proportional perturbations near good solutions instead of destructive random replacement.

C: Convergence-Based Stopping

Stop when relative improvement < 10⁻⁶ for 50 consecutive generations. Saves ~38% computation time.

9. Complexity Analysis

Time: O(G×P×N) — Original: G×P×N ops, Modified: ≈0.62×G×P×N ops
Space: O(P×N) = 50K gene pairs ≈ 0.76 MB RAM

10. Conclusion

The EOQ-seeded Modified GA outperforms the Original in both cost quality and convergence speed. EOQ seeding bridges classical analytical optimization with evolutionary search — a sound, generalizable technique. Future: multi-objective NSGA-II, parallel GA with OpenMP.
